# Skin-to-skin — awake adult, face clear

## Why this clip

HSE Ireland film, 8:05. The description names Marina Cullen, specialist midwife in lactation at the Rotunda Hospital. The film's audio shows the position on a parent and baby: tummy-down on a bare chest, a nappy, a warm blanket, head to one side, face in view. That matches the HSE newborn skin-contact page (reviewed 4 January 2026), https://www2.hse.ie/babies-children/breastfeeding/a-good-start/skin-contact/. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-11-01.

## Cues to steal

- Tummy-down on the bare chest, nappy on, warm blanket over both. Head turned to one side. Neck straight: chin off the chest, head not tipped far back. Face always visible. Nose and mouth uncovered.
- Shoulders flat on the chest. Head and arms above the breasts, legs below.
- The adult stays awake, semi-reclined, in enough light to see the face. If they feel sleepy or unwell, the baby goes in the cot until skin-to-skin can start again.
- Plan the first hour, or until after the first feed. Keep doing it through the early weeks. A birth partner can take a turn.

## Limits / do not apply when

- Tummy-down is only on an awake adult's chest. Cot sleep stays on the back — [newborn-safer-sleep](./newborn-safer-sleep.md).
- The HSE page also uses the words kangaroo care and lists neonatal-unit benefits. This note is the term-baby position with an awake adult. It is not the Global Health Media small-baby kangaroo film.
- The birth-hour page includes a hat and a nappy. The film shows a nappy and a blanket. At home, warmth is the nape check in [fi-newborn-first-days-care](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-newborn-first-days-care.md). A hat indoors is not the fix for cool hands — [dress-baby](./dress-baby.md).
- Pale, floppy, hard to wake, or grunting needs a clinician now. In Finland that call is 112.
- Latch practice stays [newborn-latch](./newborn-latch.md). Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
