# Blocked tear duct — inner-corner massage

## Why this clip

Dr James O'Donovan (MBBS; NHS-trained GP; YouTube Health shelf) demos Crigler / lacrimal-sac massage on camera: wash hands, find the inner corner, press in and down. 4:12. Points at NHS trust leaflets (Moorfields, MFT). Oxford University Hospitals also cite a shorter hospital demo as backup. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-09-13.

Backup (Cook Children's pediatrician, 1:24 — under the usual 2-minute floor): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aPXlJgZSDbA

## Cues to steal

- Watering onto the cheek, or mucus at the inner corner, is common from a few weeks. Rule out a red, swollen, painful eye first.
- Clean hands. Finger or a clean cotton swab on the **inner corner** (next to the nose), then a firm downward stroke.
- Repeat on the affected side. Wipe discharge with a warm-water pad.

## Limits / do not apply when

- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / Duodecim** wins on the day: inner-corner press **4–5 times a day**, mention it at neuvola, heavy pus needs prescribed drops — [fi-infant-blocked-tear-duct](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-blocked-tear-duct.md). This clip’s stroke count is UK/US Crigler wording, not the Finnish rule.
- Not a diagnosis. A red, swollen, or painful eye, or a baby who seems ill, is neuvola / a doctor — not more massage. Fever under 2 months always needs a clinician.
- Probing waits unless the duct has not opened by about a year. Do not apply adult DCR / tumour work-up.
- Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
