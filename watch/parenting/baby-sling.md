# Baby sling — TICKS airway check

## Why this clip

University College London Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust film on using a sling or carrier. 3:22. The publisher description teaches the TICKS check (Tight, In view, Close enough to kiss, Keep chin off the chest, Supported back) and says to follow the manufacturer instructions. NHS hospital camera, not a wrap-brand advert. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-10-11.

## Cues to steal

- Run TICKS before and while you carry: Tight, In view, Close enough to kiss, Keep chin off the chest, Supported back.
- Use that check together with the instructions for the carrier you actually own.

## Limits / do not apply when

- The film names the five words. [The Lullaby Trust sling page](https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/baby-safety/baby-product-information/slings-and-carriers/) is the longer wording of the same check: tight enough that the baby cannot slump; face visible by glancing down, without opening fabric to look; close enough to kiss the head; chin off the chest; back supported so an upright baby does not fold in half. A cradle carry faces upward, not in toward the wearer. The point is an open airway and not overheating.
- Hands-free feeding in the sling is unsafe, especially under four months, if premature, or with a health condition. Take the baby out to feed. When the feed ends, return them to the sling or put them on a clear flat sleep surface — [newborn-safer-sleep](./newborn-safer-sleep.md).
- Not a brand, a product haul, or a sleep place. Fabric, chin-on-chest, or the wearer's body blocking the mouth and nose is an emergency, not a fit to fix later.
- Neuvola wins if their carrier advice differs. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
