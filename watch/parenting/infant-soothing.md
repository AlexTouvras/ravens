# Infant soothing — crying is normal; never shake

## Why this clip

Public Health England film in partnership with ICON (*Babies cry, you can cope*), the NHS-adopted never-shake programme. 3:07. A caregiver works through a real crying spell: check needs, try comfort, then put the baby down safe and walk away. Matches week 3–4 — crying typically rises from 2 weeks toward a 6–8 week peak. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-09-06.

Backup (official AAP talking-head, 2:00): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-2-Vg5POpQ — same walk-away / ask-for-help line.

## Cues to steal

- Infant crying is normal and it will stop. Comfort can help; it will not always.
- If it is getting to you: baby on their back in a clear cot, walk away a few minutes, then come back.
- Never, ever shake. Hand the baby to another adult or call neuvola / wellbeing-services family work if you are at the end of your rope.

## Limits / do not apply when

- **UK** ICON / PHE programme. Finnish Terveyskirjasto / THL and neuvola or 112 win on the day.
- A baby **under 2 months** with **fever**, or who seems ill, always needs a clinician — [fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake.md).
- Not a colic diagnosis, a sleep-training method, or a reason to stop vitamin D.
- Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
