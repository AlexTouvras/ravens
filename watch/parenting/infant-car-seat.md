# Infant car seat — rear-facing carrier

## Why this clip

Liikenneturva film on how a baby rides in a car. 2:03. The description matches the "Vauva matkustaa turvakaukalossa" section on the page the film links ([liikenneturva.fi/lapsiautossa](https://www.liikenneturva.fi/lapsiautossa)): a rear-facing infant carrier, the carrier's own size limits, then a rear-facing child seat, back seat, and the Finnish restraint rule. Finnish road-safety body, not a seat-brand advert. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-10-18.

## Cues to steal

- A baby rides safest in a rear-facing infant carrier (turvakaukalo).
- The carrier manual says which size and age it is for. Ask the manufacturer or importer if that is unclear.
- When the carrier's weight or length limit is reached, move to a rear-facing child seat.
- The back seat is the safest place.
- Finnish law: a child restraint until 135 cm. A child under three must not travel in a car, van, or truck where an approved child restraint cannot be fitted.

## Limits / do not apply when

- The same Liikenneturva section says to set the harness to the baby before the carrier goes in the car (snug, and still comfortable), to read the manual first, and never to put a rear-facing carrier or seat on a seat with an active front airbag. A deploying front airbag is life-threatening in that seat.
- This is the Finnish rule and this carrier's manual. A US install film is a different car and a different seat.
- Lifting the carrier without twisting your back is a different film — [carry-infant-carrier](./carry-infant-carrier.md). A sling is not a car seat — [baby-sling](./baby-sling.md).
- Neuvola or the carrier manual wins if they differ on the day. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
