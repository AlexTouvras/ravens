# Bottle sterilise — wash first, then steam or boil

## Why this clip

Hillingdon Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust demo of washing and then sterilising feeding equipment. 2:35. Official trust camera, not a steriliser brand ad. Same hygiene family as boiling a dummy that keeps coming back with thrush. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-09-27.

## Cues to steal

- Wash first in hot soapy water with a bottle brush; rinse. Sterilise only after it is clean.
- Steam, boil, or a cold-water sterilising solution — follow the kit’s own instructions.
- The same wash-then-sterilise / boil habit applies to dummies and mouthed toys when yeast keeps returning.

## Limits / do not apply when

- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / Duodecim** dummy-boil wording wins on the day — [fi-infant-oral-thrush-dummy](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-oral-thrush-dummy.md). Pale tongue / cheek coatings are usually harmless thrush; neuvola advises on drops. This clip is equipment hygiene, not a diagnosis.
- Do **not** offer a dummy until latch and weight are rising — [fi-infant-milk-supply-dummy](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-milk-supply-dummy.md).
- Not a formula-prep lecture and not a product haul. Finnish infant feeding-safety stays in `knowledge/parenting/`. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
