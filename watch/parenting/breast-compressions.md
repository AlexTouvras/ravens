# Breast compressions — during a feed

## Why this clip

Kent Community Health NHS Foundation Trust film (Beside You infant feeding). 2:25. The description says the film shows breast compressions during breastfeeding so milk keeps flowing, more milk transfers, and the baby stays actively feeding. The on-film line is that you do not need compressions when the baby is already getting enough milk and gaining weight. NHS trust demo, not a pump advert. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-10-18.

## Cues to steal

- Use the compression the film shows while the baby is at the breast, so the baby keeps drinking.
- If intake and weight are already fine, skip the compression. The film says it is not required then.

## Limits / do not apply when

- This is not hand expression into a cup — [hand-expression](./hand-expression.md) — and not a pump. Do not copy a brand-pump tutorial onto this skill.
- "Enough milk" and weight still follow swallows, wet nappies, and neuvola weighing — [enough-milk](./enough-milk.md), [fi-infant-milk-supply-dummy](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-milk-supply-dummy.md). A UK helpline number on the Kent page is not the Finnish contact.
- Breast pain, a blocked duct, or mastitis is [breast-pain](./breast-pain.md). Compressing a painful breast is not the first step in that note.
- Neuvola or an infant-feeding clinic wins if they show a different hand. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
