# Read with a baby — snuggle, then follow the baby

## Why this clip

Cincinnati Children’s animation of SHARE/STEP for sharing a book with a baby. 2:43. The picture shows a caregiver and a baby with a book (on-screen labels include SHARE, Stretch, and Talk), not a lecture. The description prints the method and says the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends starting as soon as possible after birth. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-11-22.

## Cues to steal

- SHARE: Snuggle on your lap. Hands on — let the baby hold and explore the book. Show affection so reading connects with warmth. Respond to what the baby does. Enjoy it; there is no single right way.
- STEP is how to respond: Stretch word sounds. Talk about the pictures and the baby’s world. Explore sounds (a train, an animal). Be patient — reading with a baby is messy.
- Stop when interest drops. Chewing or dropping the book is part of hands-on, not a mistake to correct.

## Limits / do not apply when

- A sleepy cue or lost interest still ends the game — [infant-cues](./infant-cues.md). Do not push through a feed or a nap for a book.
- Not a minute quota, a school-readiness plan, or speech therapy. Stretching sounds here is play, not a drill.
- A clinic programme that hands out books from the **6-month** visit is a later window. This film is the from-birth sharing method.
- Any language at home is fine. Neuvola wins on the day.
- Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage. Do not compute week of life.
