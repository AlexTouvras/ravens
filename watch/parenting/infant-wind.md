# Infant wind — over-shoulder and lap burp

## Why this clip

Rotherham, Doncaster and South Humber NHS Foundation Trust health visitors (Claire) demo how to wind / burp after a feed. 7:04. Official UK health-visitor camera, not a product or colic-drop ad. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-09-27.

Backup (HSE public-health nurse Elaine Lyons, 2:10): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x_ByADce3Zo

Backup (NYU Langone maternity-nurse parent education, 4:12 — over-shoulder and lap): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m50PTFmmlxw

## Cues to steal

- Cloth on the shoulder. Baby upright on your chest, facing you; one arm under the bottom. Pat or rub between the shoulders, gently but firmly.
- Sitting on your lap: support the jaw and chest with an open hand (thumb and finger as a wedge — do not press the throat). Lean the baby slightly forward and pat the back.
- Breastfed babies often take in less air. No audible burp after about a minute is normal — continue the feed or settle.

## Limits / do not apply when

- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / Duodecim** spit-up wording wins on the day — [fi-infant-spit-up-pyloric-stenosis](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-spit-up-pyloric-stenosis.md). Spit-up is common when growth is fine. Projectile vomiting at 3 weeks–2 months needs a doctor.
- Not a colic diagnosis, a sleep-training method, or a reflux-drug plan. A baby under 2 months with fever, or who seems ill, always needs a clinician — [fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake.md).
