# Infant CPR — two fingers and rescue breaths

## Why this clip

British Red Cross training film, 4:56. It is the YouTube film on their page for a baby who is unresponsive and not breathing. The page's key action is chest compressions with two fingers and rescue breaths. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-10-25.

## Cues to steal

- Unresponsive means not moving, and no response when you call them or tap a foot. Shout for help.
- Tilt the head back slightly. Look, listen, and feel. No chest or tummy movement means not breathing.
- Someone with you calls emergency services now. Alone: one minute of breaths and compressions, then call, then continue.
- Five rescue breaths: mouth sealed over mouth and nose, gentle breaths until the chest rises.
- Thirty compressions with two fingers in the middle of the chest, firm enough that the chest goes inward, then release. Then two breaths. Repeat 30 and 2 until help arrives.
- If you cannot give breaths, do the compressions only. Stop if you realise they are breathing.

## Limits / do not apply when

- In Finland call **112**, not 999. Follow the dispatcher if the count they give is different. This is not a course certificate.
- The page is for a baby of 1 year and under. Do not use the child film (one hand) or adult compressions on a baby.
- If they are unresponsive but breathing, do not start compressions. The Red Cross breathing-baby film is a different skill and is under two minutes, so it is not catalogued here.
- Choking that is still a responsive baby is [infant-choking](./infant-choking.md). A shorter key-steps cut of this skill is `UquZ0ePjowM` (2:03). St John Ambulance `avYRvVHAvfM` (3:05) is a different publisher — do not mix its wording into these counts.
