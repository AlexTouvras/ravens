# Infant bath — cold first, elbow check, never leave

## Why this clip

National NHS midwife Chantelle Winstanley shows a baby bath: cold water first, then hot; about 8–10 cm; elbow check; top-and-tail face and nappy area; two-hand hold under neck and bottom. 2:03. Official public-health demo, not a product haul. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-09-13.

## Cues to steal

- Room warm. Everything in reach before you start. Never leave the baby in water, even for a second.
- Cold tap first, then hot. Elbow should feel body-warm. Fresh cotton wool per eye.
- Support head and neck. Babies are slippery on the way out. Dry the creases.

## Limits / do not apply when

- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / Duodecim** skin / navel wording wins on the day — [fi-infant-skin-navel-diaper-rash](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-skin-navel-diaper-rash.md). A daily bath is not required; too much washing dries the skin.
- Keep the navel fold clean and dry. Red-and-warm skin around the navel, or any blister / pus, is same-day care.
- Not a swim lesson, a product haul, or toddler bath play. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
