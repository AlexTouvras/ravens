# Blocked nose — bulb and tube suction

## Why this clip

UC Davis Health film. 5:17. Nurse manager Krystle Guenther demonstrates two ways to clear a baby's nose: the bulb syringe hospitals send home, and a filtered tube aspirator (a NoseFrida is the example on the bench). She shows how far the tip goes, how to empty it, and how to wash both tools. Children's hospital demo, and she says other brands exist. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-10-18.

## Cues to steal

- Young babies breathe through the nose. A blocked nose gets in the way of feeding.
- Bulb: baby on the back, somewhere they cannot roll. Squeeze the bulb empty away from the baby, seal the nostril just far enough, and let the bulb refill. Empty it onto a tissue before the other side. Do not push the tip in as far as it will go, and do not squirt mucus back at the baby.
- Tube aspirator: filter stays in. Tapered tip just into the nostril, breathe out, then suck. The other nostril can follow before you take it apart. Change the filter after that use.
- Wash both in warm soapy water. Rinse the bulb with clear water so no soap is left. Let them dry.

## Limits / do not apply when

- The NoseFrida is the brand on the bench, not a required purchase. The bulb is the tool she shows first.
- Saline drops are a different film (Irwin Army Community Hospital, pediatrician Eva Ewers, `qPYSP0YrahA`, 3:09). Do not invent a drop count from this UC Davis demo.
- Fever under 2 months always needs a doctor — [fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake.md). Feeding trouble together with breathing trouble is same-day care — [fi-infant-rsv-bronchiolitis-same-day](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-rsv-bronchiolitis-same-day.md). More suction is not that visit.
- Neuvola wins if they teach a different clear-the-nose step. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
