# Breastfeeding positions — recline, cross-cradle, underarm

## Why this clip

Global Health Media’s English breastfeeding-series demo of how the hold changes attachment: baby facing you, head and body in a line, then recline, cross-cradle, underarm, and side-lying. 9:27. Same public-health channel as the latch and hand-expression notes. Camera shows the skill. Confirmed via YouTube oEmbed 2026-09-27.

## Cues to steal

- Hold the baby securely, facing you, nose opposite the nipple. Ear, shoulder, and hip in a line — neck not twisted.
- Recline: lean back enough that the baby’s whole body rests on you; useful after a caesarean if the baby sits away from the incision.
- Cross-cradle: forearm takes the weight; thumb and fingers around the neck so the head can tip back. Hug in as the mouth opens wide.
- If a hand is between you, move it down so the bodies stay close.

## Limits / do not apply when

- This is the hold, not the latch gape — use [newborn-latch](./newborn-latch.md) for attaching. Pain that stays after the first seconds still needs neuvola / a midwife.
- A tight-looking tongue-tie waits for latch practice; do not cut preventively — [fi-infant-tongue-tie-wait-latch](../../knowledge/parenting/fi-infant-tongue-tie-wait-latch.md).
- Formula / mixed feeding is a valid plan. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
