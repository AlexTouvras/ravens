# NSCA — no menstrual-cycle-synced periodization needed for female athletes

## Guidance

- Apply the **same basic strength-and-conditioning principles** (progressive overload, weekly volume/frequency, individualized load) to female and male trainees — do not run a separate, lower-bar prescription "for women."
- Do **not** sync training phases, volume, or intensity to menstrual-cycle phase by default — current evidence reviewed by the NSCA does not support it as a general rule.
- Drive programming decisions by **individual goals, training history, and movement competency**, the same inputs already used for the existing ACSM weekly-volume/hypertrophy guidance.
- This is additive confirmation, not a replacement: keep using [acsm-weekly-volume-hypertrophy](./acsm-weekly-volume-hypertrophy.md) for the actual weekly set/frequency targets.

## Rationale

The NSCA's Position Statement (Part II), published in the *Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research* 40(8) (August 2026 issue), is the professional body's current position on strength-and-conditioning of female athletes — a durable, evidence-reviewed baseline rather than a single study's headline. It rules out an alternative approach (cycle-synced periodization) that otherwise shows up in fitness content, which is itself durable, actionable guidance for programming decisions.

## Limits / do not apply when

- **Pregnancy / postpartum** — keep using [acog-pregnancy-postpartum-activity](./acog-pregnancy-postpartum-activity.md); this note is about non-pregnant strength-and-conditioning prescription, not obstetric activity limits.
- Individual athletes reporting symptoms (e.g. significant menstrual-related performance or pain issues) still warrant individualized clinical/coaching adjustment — "no general rule" is not "ignore how an individual feels."
- Elite/competitive periodization research specific to a sport is out of scope; this is general strength-and-conditioning guidance, not sport-specific peaking.
- Re-check if the NSCA or a comparable body (ACSM, IOC) issues a revised position with different evidence weighting.
