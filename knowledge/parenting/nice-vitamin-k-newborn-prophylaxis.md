# Expect vitamin K prophylaxis to be offered for the newborn at/around birth

## Guidance

- Expect a care team to **offer vitamin K prophylaxis** for the newborn around the time of birth, to prevent vitamin K deficiency bleeding — treat this as a standard checklist item for the hospital stay / immediate post-birth window, not an optional extra to research from scratch.
- It is reasonable to ask the delivering unit *how* they give it (oral vs intramuscular, timing/dose schedule) rather than assume a specific protocol — NICE's guideline states the recommendation to offer it, not a single universal administration method.
- Treat a decline or deferral as a decision to raise explicitly with the care team, not a silent default.

## Rationale

NICE's living intrapartum care guideline (NG235, recommendations 1.11.12–1.11.13, last reviewed 9 Jun 2026) reinstated the advice to offer all newborns vitamin K prophylaxis, after NICE observed declining uptake once the recommendation had been dropped from an earlier postnatal-care guideline on the assumption the practice was already standard. That reinstatement is a signal the underlying practice is not automatically applied everywhere it should be — worth confirming rather than assuming, even where (as in Finland) it is separately established practice.

## Limits / do not apply when

- Applies at `age-0-72h` (labor / immediate newborn window).
- Source is a **UK (NICE)** guideline; the specific administration route, dose, and timing schedule referenced by NICE may not match Finnish (neuvola/hospital) protocol — use this note as a "confirm it happened" checklist item, not as instructions to follow the NICE dosing schedule directly. Confirm the local (Finnish) protocol with the delivering hospital.
- Not medical advice for a specific newborn's clotting risk, prematurity, or liver condition — a clinician makes that call.
- Do not use this note to infer `Child born` or change **Current stage** in [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) — a human sets that after birth.
