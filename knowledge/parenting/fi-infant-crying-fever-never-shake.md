# Finland — crying typically rises from 2 weeks to about 1½ months; fever under 2 months always needs a doctor

## Guidance

- Crying usually **increases from 2 weeks of age until about 1½ months**, then eases. The page calls this the colic-age window; “three-month colic” is usually gone by **four months**. Amount is individual.
- Respond to **early hunger cues** (mouth opening, fist-to-mouth, sucking fingers). Waiting for a full cry is a late hunger sign and can make feeding harder.
- A baby **under 2 months** with **fever**, or who seems ill to the parents, **always needs a clinician’s assessment**. A household virus does not cancel that rule.
- **Vitamin D drops do not cause crying**, even though they often start in the same window as the fussing rise — keep [fi-infant-vitamin-d-from-2-weeks](./fi-infant-vitamin-d-from-2-weeks.md).
- **Never shake** a baby. If a parent is at the end of their rope, hand the baby to another adult, step away, and contact neuvola or wellbeing-services family work. If a baby has been shaken, take them to a doctor.

## Rationale

Terveyskirjasto’s living crying/soothing page is based on THL *Meille tulee vauva* (2023). The 2-week-to-1½-month rise, the under-2-month fever rule, the vitamin-D-is-not-the-cause line, and the never-shake / ask-for-help rule are standing public-health items — they hold if any single day’s headline is deleted.

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: **2 weeks through about 4 months** (`age-2-8w` now; fever rule is **under 2 months**). Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage**.
- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / THL** wording. Neuvola or 112 wins on the day.
- Not a colic diagnosis, a sleep-training method, or a reason to stop vitamin D. Inconsolable crying with poor feeding or no weight gain still goes to neuvola.
- Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
