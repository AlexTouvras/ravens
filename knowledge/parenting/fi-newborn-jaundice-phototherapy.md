# Finland — newborn jaundice is common; phototherapy is the standard next step

## Guidance

- Up to **half of term newborns** (and even more preterm newborns) become jaundiced in the first days of life. Expect yellow skin plus a listless, easily tired baby — that pairing is described as typical, not automatically an emergency.
- **Physiological jaundice** peaks at **3–5 days** of age. Breast-milk jaundice occurs in about **10%** of newborns through **4–6 weeks**.
- Jaundice **before 2 days** of age is more often caused by blood-group immunization (ABO or Rh), infection, large bruises, or a birth lump — flag it to the ward rather than waiting for the physiological peak.
- Bilirubin can be screened with a **skin meter** on the maternity ward; a blood sample is taken if needed.
- When the level exceeds the **age- and gestational-week phototherapy threshold**, treatment is **blue-light phototherapy** plus adequate feeding/fluids, using **1–3** lamps or mattresses, with duration set by the bilirubin level.
- Visible jaundice after going home still goes first to the **discharging ward** in [fi-newborn-hospital-discharge](./fi-newborn-hospital-discharge.md) — phototherapy is the standard next step the ward will decide, not a rare last resort.

## Rationale

Terveyskylä Naistalo's living newborn-jaundice page (last reviewed 16 Jan 2026) is current Finnish-hospital protocol. Prevalence, the 3–5-day peak, the before-2-days red flag, and phototherapy as the usual treatment are standing checklist items — they hold if any single day's headline is deleted and they fill the gap the discharge note only flagged (call the ward for visible jaundice).

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: `age-0-72h` (adjacent prepare-now) through the **3–5-day** peak and any phototherapy stay, plus breast-milk jaundice through **4–6 weeks**. Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage** — a human sets `Child born`, not this note.
- **Finnish hospital / Terveyskylä Naistalo** national guidance. Individual units set their own phototherapy thresholds; the discharging ward's numbers win on the day.
- Not a diagnosis or a substitute for the discharging ward or 112. Sleepy-and-will-not-feed, inconsolable crying, or fever still follow [fi-newborn-hospital-discharge](./fi-newborn-hospital-discharge.md) red flags.
- Does not replace the pediatrician discharge exam, the 2–5-day heel-prick, or the statutory neuvola home visit.
