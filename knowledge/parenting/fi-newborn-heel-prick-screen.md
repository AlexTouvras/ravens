# Finland — newborn rare-disease heel-prick at 2–5 days, with parental consent

## Guidance

- Rare congenital metabolic and immunodeficiency diseases are **recommended to be screened in all newborns** so treatable conditions can be caught while babies are still often symptom-free.
- A few drops of blood are taken from the **heel** as a blood-spot sample (VasSeu) on the maternity ward or at a laboratory sampling point when the baby is **2–5 days** old. The sample is described as harmless to the baby.
- Screening **requires the parents' consent** — expect a consent ask before or just after going home, not an automatic draw.
- Put this on the arrival checklist next to the pediatrician discharge exam in [fi-newborn-hospital-discharge](./fi-newborn-hospital-discharge.md): if discharge is before day 2, the sample may be taken at a laboratory point instead of on the ward.

## Rationale

Terveyskylä's living newborn-screening page (last reviewed 17 Oct 2025) is current Finnish-hospital protocol. The 2–5-day window, heel-prick method, and parental-consent requirement are standing checklist items — they hold if any single day's headline is deleted and they fill a gap the hub did not yet cover (discharge follow-up, neuvola home visit, vitamin K, HUS call-first, and safe sleep).

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: `age-0-72h` (adjacent prepare-now) through the **2–5-day** sample. Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage** — a human sets `Child born`, not this note.
- **Finnish hospital / Terveyskylä** national guidance. Individual units may take the sample on the ward or at a lab point; the discharging ward's own instructions win on the day.
- Not a diagnosis and not a substitute for the discharging ward. Consent can be declined; this note does not argue for or against that choice.
- Does not replace [fi-newborn-hospital-discharge](./fi-newborn-hospital-discharge.md) jaundice / first-days red flags or the statutory neuvola home visit.
