# Finland — start short awake tummy time now; parent chest or lap first, then the floor

## Guidance

- Tummy-lying is the starting position for later movement. Practise from the beginning on **varied surfaces**, a little at a time.
- With a young baby, start on the **parent’s chest** (skin-to-skin) or **carried prone on the lap**. Place the baby on the **floor** more regularly as they grow.
- At first the baby only tolerates **very short spells**. Quality beats duration — every second of a pleasant session counts.
- Support the arms near the body at about **90 degrees** so the centre of gravity sits nearer the **navel than the shoulders**. A pillow or a parent’s arm/shoulder/leg can unload the head if the baby does not enjoy the floor yet.
- This is **awake play**, never a sleep position. Back-sleep stays in [aap-safe-sleep-abcs](./aap-safe-sleep-abcs.md) and [fi-newborn-first-days-care](./fi-newborn-first-days-care.md).

## Rationale

Terveyskirjasto’s living tummy-time page is current Finnish how-to: start early, progress from chest/lap to floor, and keep sessions short and pleasant. That survives headline churn and is the week-3 household rule — not a US minute-count.

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: **from the first days through early infancy** (`age-2-8w` now). Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage**.
- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto** quality-over-duration wording wins this week. AAP’s 15–30 minutes/day by 7 weeks is a later volume target — [SIG-20260906-001](../../signals/SIG-20260906-001.md) — not tonight’s quota.
- Never as a sleep position. Unsupervised tummy time, or a baby who cannot breathe freely, is out of scope.
- Not medical advice for torticollis, plagiocephaly treatment, or a baby who cannot lift the head at all — that is neuvola.
