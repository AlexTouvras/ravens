# Finland — week-3 sleep is still 2–4 hour stretches; night/day split waits for melatonin around 3 months

## Guidance

- A newborn typically sleeps about **14–16 hours per day** in roughly **2–4 hour stretches** at varying times and does **not** yet have a regular sleep–wake rhythm.
- Melatonin secretion regularises by about **3 months**, after which wake periods concentrate in the day and sleep at night.
- Before **2–3 months** there is no clear “sleep window.” Bedtime follows the baby’s cues (becoming drowsy), not a clock.
- A roughly regular daytime programme is **optional support**, not a night-training target. Self-settling practice on the same page is for about **6 months–2 years**, not week 3.

## Rationale

Terveyskirjasto’s living child-sleep page (citing THL and Duodecim 2022) is current Finnish public-health sleep development. Short cycles until melatonin organises around 3 months are a standing expectation — they hold if any sleep-training headline is deleted.

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: **newborn through about 2–3 months** (`age-2-8w` now). After melatonin regularises, use the next rows on the same page, not this note. Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage**.
- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / THL** wording. Neuvola wins if a specific sleep concern is raised.
- Do not treat a missing 6-month self-settling skill, or a missing consolidated night, as a week-3 failure. Do not start cry-it-out from this note.
