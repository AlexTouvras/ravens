# Finland — infant blocked tear ducts often start at 2–4 weeks; massage 4–5 times a day and mention it at neuvola

## Guidance

- A blocked tear duct in **small infants** often starts showing at **2–4 weeks** (watering, tears onto the cheek, mucus/pus).
- Home care: press the **inner corner of the eye** with a **clean cotton swab 4–5 times a day**. Wipe any discharge with a **warm-water cotton pad** several times a day. Repeat whenever the eye clearly waters or crusts.
- Raise watering, cheek overflow, or discharge at the **neuvola** visit. **Heavy discharge** often needs a **doctor-prescribed antibiotic-drop** course for conjunctivitis.
- An infant duct usually **opens with growth by about one year**. If it does not, neuvola refers to ophthalmology to assess **probing under anaesthesia**.
- This is the **2–4 week onset / massage / neuvola** rule, not a week-plan and not the skin / navel / diaper-rash note.

## Rationale

Duodecim Terveyskirjasto’s living “Kyyneltietukos” page (Lääkärikirja Duodecim, dated 11 Mar 2026) is standing Finnish public-health wording. The 2–4 week onset, 4–5×/day inner-corner massage, neuvola mention, heavy-discharge drops, and “usually opens by about one year” line hold if any single day’s headline is deleted.

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: infant onset typically **2–4 weeks**; watch-and-massage through the first year (`age-2-8w` now). Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage**. Do not invent a birth date or change Current stage.
- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / Duodecim** wording. Neuvola, the maternity hospital, or 112 wins on the day.
- Not a diagnosis, an antibiotic plan, or a product haul. Do **not** apply the page’s **adult** DCR / tubing / tumour work-up to an infant.
- Not the skin / navel / diaper-rash rule in [fi-infant-skin-navel-diaper-rash](./fi-infant-skin-navel-diaper-rash.md). Fever under 2 months still always needs a doctor — [fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake](./fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake.md).
- Do not treat this as a household week-plan.
