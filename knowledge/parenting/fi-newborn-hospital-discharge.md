# Finland — pediatrician discharge exam, 24–36h follow-up, and first-days red flags

## Guidance

- A **pediatrician always examines** the newborn before going home, typically at about **1–2 days** of age.
- Typical stay: first-time parents **2–3 days**; later-birth parents **1–2 days**; later after caesarean. Discharge is still individual to mother and baby.
- **Discharge under 24 hours** books a pediatrician follow-up at the birth hospital. **Discharge at 24–36 hours** books a hospital nurse visit. Both visits happen at the hospital, not at neuvola.
- Contact **neuvola preferably before leaving** to book the first clinic visit (this is in addition to the statutory home visit in [fi-neuvola-home-visit-first-time-parents](./fi-neuvola-home-visit-first-time-parents.md)).
- Visible jaundice is usually strongest on **days 4–5** — call the **discharging ward**, not wait for neuvola. Phototherapy is the standard next step, not a rare emergency — [fi-newborn-jaundice-phototherapy](./fi-newborn-jaundice-phototherapy.md).
- Call the discharging ward **immediately** if the baby is sleepy and will not wake to feed (or will not suck even when woken), is inconsolable, or has a temperature **above 38°C**. The same ward is also the first call for worry or care advice before the first neuvola check.

## Rationale

Terveyskylä Naistalo's living newborn-discharge page (last reviewed 12 Jan 2026) is current Finnish-hospital home-coming protocol. The pediatrician exam, the 24h / 24–36h follow-up split, and the first-days red flags are standing checklist items — they hold if any single day's headline is deleted and they fill a gap the hub did not yet cover (labor call-first, vitamin K, neuvola home visit, and safe sleep).

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: `age-0-72h` (adjacent prepare-now) through the first days home, including the jaundice peak on days 4–5. Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage** — a human sets `Child born`, not this note.
- **Finnish hospital / Terveyskylä Naistalo** national guidance. Individual units may book follow-ups slightly differently; the discharging ward's own instructions win on the day.
- Not a diagnosis or a substitute for the discharging ward or 112. Reduced feeding, inconsolable crying, fever, or visible jaundice still go back to that ward (or emergency services when the situation is urgent).
- Does not replace [aap-safe-sleep-abcs](./aap-safe-sleep-abcs.md) or the statutory neuvola home visit — those are separate arrival-checklist items.
