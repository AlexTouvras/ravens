# Finland — spit-up is normal when growth is fine; projectile vomiting at 3 weeks–2 months needs a doctor

## Guidance

- Spit-up during or after feeds is **normal** for newborns and infants. About **half of three-month-olds** spit up at least five times a day; abundant spit-up is still seen in **one in ten 10-month-olds** and usually stops by **12–18 months**.
- If neuvola checks show **normal weight and length**, even **abundant spit-up is usually harmless**. Watch-and-feed — not an automatic reflux work-up.
- Talk to **neuvola** about reflux *disease* only when spit-up comes with other signs: a **break in the growth curve**, **breathing pauses**, or **night-time restlessness**.
- If spit-up turns into **projectile / arc-like vomiting** during or after feeds, take the child to a **doctor the same day**. That can be **pyloric stenosis** (food cannot leave the stomach fast enough), typically **worst at three weeks to two months**: the baby still wants to eat, then vomits milk-coloured projectile vomits; ongoing vomiting can stall weight gain.
- Blood in vomit, or green bile vomit, also needs a care unit — not a “just spit-up” wait.

## Rationale

Duodecim Terveyskirjasto’s living “Pulauttelu ja oksentelu lapsella” page (Lääkärikirja Duodecim) is standing Finnish public-health wording. The normal-when-growth-is-fine line, the reflux-disease extra-sign list, and the 3-week–2-month projectile-vomit / pyloric-stenosis red flag hold if any single day’s headline is deleted. This is the spit-up / reflux-disease / pyloric-stenosis rule, not a week-plan.

## Limits / do not apply when

- Age window: spit-up reassurance is **newborn through ~12–18 months**; the pyloric-stenosis red flag is **typically worst at 3 weeks–2 months** (`age-2-8w` now). Re-check [`knowledge/parenting/README.md`](./README.md) **Current stage**.
- **Finnish Terveyskirjasto / Duodecim** wording. Neuvola or 112 wins on the day.
- Not a diagnosis, a reflux-drug plan, or a reason to start complementary food early. The source also lists thickeners, incline, and left-side sleep as possible spit-up reducers — **do not apply left-side sleep**; keep [aap-safe-sleep-abcs](./aap-safe-sleep-abcs.md) back-sleep.
- Poor feeding, no weight gain, fever, or a baby who seems ill still goes to neuvola / a doctor (see [fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake](./fi-infant-crying-fever-never-shake.md) and [thl-0-1-month-development-growth](./thl-0-1-month-development-growth.md)).
