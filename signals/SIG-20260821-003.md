# Sprint intervals reshape cardiometabolic blood proteins far more than moderate cycling

## Why open

A Rockefeller-led trial (Cell Reports Medicine, 13 Aug 2026) found six 30-second all-out sprint intervals altered nearly a quarter of measured blood proteins immediately post-exercise — including 32 of 33 proteins linked to lower type-2-diabetes and obesity risk in UK Biobank data — versus under 0.25% of proteins changed by 90 minutes of continuous moderate cycling. Interesting support for keeping a short, time-efficient sprint-interval session in the weekly mealplan `lean` template, distinct from (not a replacement for) existing strength-volume guidance — but it is a single acute-biomarker study, not yet a durable programming rule.

## Watch for

- Corroborating studies or exercise-physiology body commentary (ACSM-class) on sprint-interval minimum effective dose for cardiometabolic health
- A durable "add one short sprint-interval session per week" rule with clear limits — promote to knowledge if corroborated
- Do not treat acute protein changes alone as proven long-term health-outcome data
