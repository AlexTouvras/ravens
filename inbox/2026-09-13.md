- **title:** Terveyskirjasto: postpartum mastitis can stay home-care if afebrile; fever or no ease in 12 hours of emptying needs a doctor
- **claim:** Duodecim Terveyskirjasto’s living “Rintatulehdus” page (Lääkärikirja Duodecim, dated 31 Mar 2026) says postpartum mastitis occurs in 3–6% of breastfeeding mothers, usually after a blocked duct or nipple cracks, most often staphylococcal. A blocked duct starts as a tender, sharply bordered red patch; as it worsens, fever can rise to about 40 °C and the whole breast becomes red and sore. If there is no fever, emptying the breast is enough — feed first from the sore side and pump if needed; a cool pack between feeds eases swelling. Start antibiotics promptly if fever has risen (typically 10–14 days of flucloxacillin, cephalexin, or roxithromycin); the baby may keep feeding from the inflamed breast, including during antibiotics, and the milk remains suitable. If fever is high or symptoms do not ease within 12 hours of frequent emptying, contact a doctor or health centre to assess antibiotics (can be prescribed by phone when symptoms are short). Afebrile burning-nipple mastitis can be Candida — then also check the baby’s mouth and treat both. An abscess (ongoing fever plus a separate tender lump despite antibiotics) needs drainage. This finding is the emptying / 12-hour / fever rule, not a week-plan and not the already-filed milk-sufficiency note.
