### parenting

#### FIND-20260909-002

- **title:** Terveyskirjasto: spit-up is normal when growth is fine; projectile vomiting at 3 weeks–2 months may be pyloric stenosis
- **claim:** Duodecim Terveyskirjasto’s living “Pulauttelu ja oksentelu lapsella” page (Lääkärikirja Duodecim, dated 10 May 2023) says spit-up during or after feeds is normal for all newborns and infants, and even abundant spit-up is usually harmless when neuvola checks show normal weight and length; infant reflux *disease* is named only when spit-up comes with other signs such as a break in the growth curve, breathing pauses, or night-time restlessness. About half of three-month-olds spit up at least five times a day; abundant spit-up is still seen in one in ten 10-month-olds and usually stops by 12–18 months. If spit-up turns into projectile vomiting during or after feeds, take the child to a doctor — that can be pyloric stenosis (food cannot leave the stomach fast enough), typically worst at three weeks to two months of age: the baby still wants to eat, then vomits milk-coloured projectile vomits, and ongoing vomiting can stall weight gain. This finding is the spit-up / reflux-disease / pyloric-stenosis rule, not a week-plan.
- **why_it_matters:** household (`age-2-8w`) — a Finnish red-flag checklist in the 3-week–2-month window: abundant spit-up plus normal growth stays watch-and-feed; growth break / apnea / night restlessness is reflux-disease talk for neuvola; projectile vomiting is same-day doctor, not “just spit-up”
