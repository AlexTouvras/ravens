#### FIND-20260921-003

- **title:** Terveyskirjasto: infant bronchiolitis is usually RSV; under-3-month feeding or breathing trouble is same-day care
- **claim:** Duodecim Terveyskirjasto’s living “Ilmatiehyttulehdus (bronkioliitti) lapsella” page (Lääkärikirja Duodecim, dated 11 Mar 2024) defines bronchiolitis as small-airway inflammation in children under 1 year, most often RSV, peaking in winter epidemics. The hardest age is 1–6 months; infants under 3 months and those born preterm are a special risk group, and a large share of under-3-month cases need hospital care. Fast breathing, worsening work of breathing, and poor feeding are reasons to take a small infant to a doctor; during epidemic season any under-6-month infant with feeding *and* breathing trouble — especially under 3 months — should always be assessed, because even a mild RSV infection often becomes a hospital stay. Hospital care is typical: support breathing, clear mucus, give oxygen. Two RSV products exist (a pregnancy vaccine and a newborn antibody injection, each ~6 months of cover) but the page says national recommendations are still being prepared. This finding is the under-3-month RSV / same-day-care rule, not a week-plan and not the already-filed under-2-month fever / never-shake note.
- **why_it_matters:** household (`age-2-8w`) — a Finnish autumn/winter red-flag for the weeks when RSV bronchiolitis is most dangerous: fast or hard breathing plus poor feeding in a small infant is same-day care, not watch-and-wait at home
