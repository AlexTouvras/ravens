#### FIND-20260914-001

- **title:** Terveyskirjasto: a tight-looking infant tongue-tie is not a preventive cut; give latch practice time; lip-tie does not change breastfeeding mechanics
- **claim:** Duodecim Terveyskirjasto’s living “Vauvan kireä kielijänne ja huulijänne” page (Lääkärikirja Duodecim, dated 3 Mar 2025) says the lingual frenulum is a normal floor-of-mouth fold with wide appearance variation (published prevalence 0–12%; no shared diagnostic criteria). A newborn frenulum often looks tight without affecting eating or life — then no procedure. Surgery assessment is only when an unusually tight frenulum clearly limits tongue movement *and* there is a latch problem or breastfeeding pain; give time and support for latch practice first. Do not cut a tongue-tie or lip-tie in infancy to prevent later eating, sleep, crying, spit-up, speech, or bite problems — studies have not shown that benefit. “Posterior tongue-tie” is poorly defined and must not be a surgical indication. An infant upper-lip frenulum is a normal structure, does not affect breastfeeding mechanics, and does not need cutting to improve latch. If a cut is done, the infant can feed immediately; do not massage the mouth scar (oral-aversion risk). This finding is the tongue-tie / lip-tie / wait-on-practice rule, not a week-plan and not the already-filed milk-sufficiency / dummy-wait note.

#### FIND-20260914-002

- **title:** Terveyskirjasto: milk-only infants may stool several times a day or only every 5–7 days; straining then a normal stool is not constipation
- **claim:** Duodecim Terveyskirjasto’s living “Lapsen normaali ulostaminen ja suolen toiminta” page (Lääkärikirja Duodecim, dated 1 Nov 2022) says milk-only stool colour can run yellow, brown, or even green-black, and breastfed infants may pass stool several times a day or as seldom as every 5–7 days. Typical milk stool is yellowish with “milk grains.” A baby who strains, grunts, or even cries for 5–10 minutes and then passes a *normal* stool is not constipated — the pelvic-floor muscles are still immature and the infant cannot yet relax them on cue; this eases with age without treatment. Complementary-food stool firming at 4–6 months and toddler constipation / toddler diarrhoea are older-child rules and are not this finding. See a doctor for unintended weight loss, blood or clots in stool, stool that turns black, fever or lasting unusual tiredness, growth break, repeated vomiting or blood in vomit, ongoing hard-to-live-with belly pain, or continuous watery diarrhoea. This finding is the milk-only frequency / straining-is-not-constipation rule, not a week-plan and not the already-filed spit-up / pyloric-stenosis note.
